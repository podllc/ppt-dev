## Service Models

### 1. Airlift - Embedded

**Target Audience:**
Midsize to large organizations with existing technology teams that have a large investment in older technologies and are struggling to move to modern development and deployment practices.

**Approach:**
- Embed within an existing team
- Provide hands-on technical leadership and mentoring
- Become part of your team, leading by example
- Ship code and deploy applications

**Ideal Engagement:**
- 1-2+ senior technologists for 6+ months
- Can start smaller to prove value
- Optionally includes 1-2 user experience leaders

**Success Factors:**
- Buy-in at the executive level
- Acceptance across the technical team
- Cannot affect change without support and acceptance

---

### 2. Airlift - Project Based

**Target Audience:**
Organizations with discrete business needs with a defined scope, budget, and timeline where it makes sense to bring in an outside consultancy for soup-to-nuts design through delivery to support.

**Approach:**
Deploy using modern technologies and best practices. Create reproducible templates and patterns for internal teams to adopt.

**The Three-Project Progression:**

**Project 1: We build it**
- We do the work while you observe
- Create templates, CI/CD pipelines, design patterns
- Ship production-ready software
- Document our approach thoroughly

**Project 2: We build it with you**
- Your team works alongside us
- Hands-on knowledge transfer as we collaborate
- You contribute code, we review and guide
- Shared ownership of delivery

**Project 3: You build it with our support**
- Your team leads the work
- We provide code reviews and architecture guidance
- Ensure quality and consistency
- Validate you're ready to own the work

**Ideal Engagement:**
- 3-6 months across three projects
- Projects broken into phases to deliver incremental business value earlier
- Team size: 1-2 UX leaders and 2-3 senior technologists

**Why This Works:**
You don't just get working software—you get the capability to replicate it. After three projects, you own the practices, the patterns, and the expertise. No vendor dependency.

## Disciplines & Maturity Models

### 1. Process & Product

#### Areas Addressed:
- Lack of clearly articulated and defined strategic business goals
- Lack of organizational backlog of initiatives aligned to strategic goals
- Poor visibility into R&D (Product & Engineering) work
- Alignment to strategic goals
- Current progress and delivery timelines

#### Approach:
Implement practical and effective Agile processes without being dogmatic, always focused on business results. Keep teams aligned to goals and give leaders/stakeholders situational awareness to make informed decisions on scope and priority to maximize value delivery.

#### Maturity Model Levels:

**Base:**
- No organizational backlog or strategy
- Poorly defined "Products"
- Responsibility not associated with owners
- No structured planning process
- Work is event-driven
- Ad-hoc processes

**Beginner:**
- Has loose organizational backlog
- Has an organizational strategy
- Has defined products with teams for each
- Ad hoc or yearly planning processes
- Some transparency but delayed and/or unreliable

**Intermediate:**
- Well-defined product backlog
- Able to prioritize versus strategy
- Organizational capacity loosely aligned to product needs
- Consistent transparency into work
- Teams own products
- Product teams have internal processes aligned to reduce dependencies

**Advanced:**
- Backlogs regularly reprioritized based on market evidence and strategic goals
- Org capacity and product roadmaps aligned to maximize business value
- Releases happen at end of each planning cycle
- Releases are regular and reliable with respect to time/scope
- Stakeholder feedback is regular

**Expert:**
- Has T-shaped teams able to work across multiple products
- Releases happen each sprint
- Teams have dimensions of product expertise
- Release is independent of planning horizons (continuous releases)
- Transparency is complete, timely, and aids in strategic decision making

#### Design Phase Activities:
- Fully detailed backlog for first 8 weeks with sprint-by-sprint goals for release

#### Development Phase Activities:
- Updated backlog with newly discovered work, reviewed with client weekly
- Updated sprint and release plans based on progress of team
- Essential program metrics

#### Support Phase Activities:
- Continued involvement in ceremonial events of the agile process
- Feedback to teams and leads on backlog management, process improvement, and managing metrics

### 2. DevOps

#### Summary:
Moving to cloud enables enterprises to more rapidly develop and deploy new applications but comes with operational and financial changes to most enterprise IT teams. Pod provides services that leverage all the benefits of developing new products in the cloud while sustaining high levels of governance and control over process and spend.

#### Areas Addressed:
- Lack of ownership of code from development to production
- Long timelines to deployed code
- Heterogeneous, unaudited infrastructure
- Non-standardized deployment strategies
- Optimization of resource utilization moving from CapEx/OnPrem to OpEx/Cloud

#### Maturity Model Assessment Areas:

**Level 1:** No capabilities or assets

**Level 2:** Isolated capabilities, no central strategy or set of assets

**Level 3:** Some capabilities, strategies vary by team/product

**Level 4:** Widespread capabilities, canonical strategies, limited sharing of assets

**Level 5:** Institutionalized capabilities with canonical strategies and reusable assets across all teams

**Areas of Assessment:**
- Auditability of Infrastructure
- Build and Test Automation
- Deployment Automation
- Automated Systems Monitoring and Telemetry
- Reuse/Codification of Infrastructure
- Configuration and Secrets Management

#### Assessment Phase Activities:
- Infrastructure/delivery assessment of current state
- Cloud/Hybrid/OnPrem future state model

#### Design Phase Activities:
- Source control systems set up
- Cloud infrastructure design of supporting systems
- Cloud accounts provisioned

#### Development Phase Activities:
- Coded infrastructure in source control
- Dev, Staging, and Production environments
- All needed build pipelines, including test automation

#### Support Phase Activities:
- Collaborative maintenance and training for client DevOps/infrastructure teams
- Guidance on cloud cost management

### 3. UI & UX

#### Areas Addressed:
- Lack of consistency across applications
- Impact on users (steep and constant learning curves, lower morale)
- Impact on development (takes more time/dollars, reinventing wheel)
- "Common mistakes" that result from poor UX
- Time/cost for tech support and user training
- Missed opportunities for higher productivity and throughput
- Missed opportunities for doing "smarter tasks" that add business value

#### Assessment Phase Activities:
- Discovery findings from facilitated stakeholder meetings to understand current state, business challenges, and goals
- Expert user experience assessment to benchmark software against usability and accessibility standards
- Usability assessment and/or user observations to yield prioritized issues and opportunities lists
- User stories based on personas or user roles (permissions)

#### Design Phase Activities:
- Application design in clickable prototype that can be shared and usability tested with developers and stakeholders
- Iterative, collaborative check-ins to ensure viability of solution
- Evolving design pattern library and style guide
- Approved screens for 3-5 epics for handoff to development team
- Backlog of features documented and prioritized

#### Development Phase Activities:
- Usability testing to inform refinements and client acceptance
- Incremental changes to designs based on the above
- Final "UX QA" test pass before release

#### Support Phase Activities:
- Updates to patterns and style guides based on product feedback from users
- Enhancements and new designs to support the full roadmap (as needed)
- Note: The design pattern library is intended to avoid new design work post-release; however, Pod can be available if new patterns or unique pages are necessary
