Your technology works. Sort of. But it's slow, expensive, and nobody under 40 wants to maintain it. Your team knows things need to change, but they're buried in feature requests and firefighting.

## The four disciplines

### UI & UX: Design systems that scale

**The problem:**
Every application looks different. Learning curves are steep. Developers design interfaces because you don't have UX resources. Support costs are high. User satisfaction is low.

**What we do:**
- Usability assessments and expert UX reviews
- Clickable prototypes validated with real users
- Design pattern libraries and style guides
- Component reuse across applications
- Accessibility and standards compliance

**What you get:**
Consistent user experiences. Faster development (stop reinventing wheels). Lower training costs. Higher user satisfaction. Applications that work together instead of against each other.

**Example deliverables:**
- Pattern library with 50+ reusable components
- Style guide maintained by your team
- Validated designs for 3-5 epics per 8-week cycle
- Usability test results informing refinements

---

### DevOps: Ship faster, break less

**The problem:**
Deployments take weeks. Nobody knows what's running in production. Cloud costs are spiraling. Infrastructure is heterogeneous and unaudited. Testing is manual. Every release is an adventure.

**What we do:**
- Infrastructure as code (Terraform)
- CI/CD pipelines with automated testing
- Containerized deployment (Kubernetes, Docker)
- Cloud infrastructure design and cost management
- Monitoring, telemetry, and observability

**What you get:**
Deployments in hours, not weeks. Auditable infrastructure. Predictable releases. Cost transparency. Your team owns the pipelines and can maintain them.

**Maturity progression:**
- **Level 1**: No automation, manual deployments
- **Level 2**: Some automation, inconsistent strategies
- **Level 3**: Team-specific practices, limited asset sharing
- **Level 4**: Canonical strategies, reusable assets
- **Level 5**: Institutionalized practices across all teams

We meet you at your level and move you up systematically.

**Example deliverables:**
- Coded infrastructure in source control
- Dev, staging, and production environments
- Build pipelines with automated tests
- Cloud cost management guidance

---

### Process & Product: Agile that works

**The problem:**
No organizational backlog. Strategic goals don't connect to R&D work. Planning is ad-hoc. Stakeholders have no visibility into progress. Teams are reactive, not proactive.

**What we do:**
- Product backlog aligned to strategic goals
- Organizational capacity planning
- Transparent progress tracking
- Practical Agile processes (not dogma)
- Regular stakeholder communication

**What you get:**
Teams aligned to goals. Leaders with situational awareness. Predictable delivery timelines. Scope and priority decisions based on data, not politics.

**Maturity progression:**
- **Base**: No backlog, ad-hoc work, event-driven
- **Beginner**: Loose backlog, yearly planning, delayed transparency
- **Intermediate**: Well-defined backlog, consistent transparency, owned products
- **Advanced**: Regular reprioritization, reliable releases, stakeholder feedback loops
- **Expert**: T-shaped teams, continuous releases, complete transparency

We're pragmatic. If it helps you ship, we do it. If it's ceremony for ceremony's sake, we skip it.

**Example deliverables:**
- Sprint-by-sprint goals for 8-week cycles
- Updated backlogs reviewed weekly
- Essential program metrics
- Ceremonial process refinements

---

### Architecture: Technology choices that last

**The problem:**
Technology stack decisions made five years ago. Nobody knows why. Teams can't translate business problems into technical solutions efficiently. Technical debt is crushing velocity.

**What we do:**
- Platform assessments
- Technology stack recommendations
- Domain and data modeling
- API design
- QA and testing strategy
- Pattern application (CQRS, Event Sourcing, Saga, etc.)

**What you get:**
Modern stack that supports business needs. Clear architectural patterns. Documentation that developers actually use. Reduced time from idea to implementation.

**Example deliverables:**
- Target tech stack for future development
- Domain models and API endpoint designs
- Production-ready software every 2 weeks
- Collaborative feature development with your team

We're not religious about technology. Angular vs React? .NET vs Node? We choose based on your team's skills, your existing investments, and your business needs—not what we want on our resumes.

## How Traditional Airlift works

### Assessment (1-2 weeks)

We assess your current state across all four disciplines using maturity models. You get:

- Diagnostic assessment with maturity ratings
- Cost and risk analysis
- Defined plan with 1-3 prioritized projects
- 8-week business cycle roadmap

We involve your team: stakeholder interviews, workshops, self-assessments. Transformation requires understanding where you are and where you're going.

### Design (Weeks 1-2 of each 8-week cycle)

Detailed planning per discipline:

- **UI/UX**: Approved designs for 3-5 epics
- **DevOps**: Cloud infrastructure design, accounts provisioned
- **Process**: Fully detailed backlog with sprint goals
- **Architecture**: Domain models, API designs, test plans

### Development (Weeks 3-8 of each cycle)

Production-ready deliveries every 2 weeks:

- **UI/UX**: Usability testing, design refinements, final QA
- **DevOps**: Environments deployed, pipelines running, infrastructure coded
- **Process**: Updated backlogs, sprint reviews, metrics tracking
- **Architecture**: Working software, documentation, QA testing

### Support (Ongoing)

Knowledge transfer and collaborative maintenance:

- **UI/UX**: Pattern library updates based on feedback
- **DevOps**: Training for your infrastructure teams, cost guidance
- **Process**: Participation in Agile ceremonies, process improvements
- **Architecture**: Collaborative feature development, bug fixes

By the end of the engagement, your team owns the practices, the patterns, and the tools.

## Maturity models in action

We use maturity models to assess where you are and plan where you're going. Here's an example from the **Process & Product** discipline:

**Base:** No backlog, ad-hoc work, event-driven, no structure

**Beginner:** Loose backlog, some strategy, yearly planning, unreliable transparency

**Intermediate:** Defined backlog, consistent transparency, teams own products, reduced dependencies

**Advanced:** Regular reprioritization, reliable releases, stakeholder feedback, capacity aligned to goals

**Expert:** T-shaped teams, continuous releases, complete transparency, strategic decision-making

Most organizations start at Base or Beginner. We move them to Intermediate or Advanced in 6-12 months. Expert is rare and requires sustained commitment—but it's where velocity really takes off.
